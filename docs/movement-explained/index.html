How to interpret these examples

These are the same illustrative players used in the original comparison charts, selected for typical recorded duration, not performance. Speed is converted to meters/second and smoothed over five frames. Movement starts at three consecutive frames at or above 1 yard/second. Paths are translated and rotated to a common initial direction, without reflection. Path axes are in yards and preserve equal spatial scale.

A and B identify the largest positive and negative speed change over any half-second window after movement onset. They are descriptive highlights, not the earlier duration-qualified acceleration effort counts, and may overlap or occur during the drill exit. If there is no positive or negative change, the respective marker is omitted. They do not establish the intended cut or body technique. End means the end of recording, not a verified drill finish. We must verify drill phases before using these as scouting measures.
